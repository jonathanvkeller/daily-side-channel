# Daily Side-Channel
**October 27, 2026 · No. 028**

*A two-and-a-half-hour mixtape made with Roma youth in Rotherham, a household notebook written from both ends, and eight beats that somehow contain every possible three-beat phrase.*

Today's question: **what if the structure of the object is already the story?**

---

## THE SIGNAL · no major signal today

**No major signal today.** There is, however, a recent underground release whose economics are considerably more interesting than another limited-edition cassette announcement.

## LISTENING ROOM · the mixtape that wants to build its own studio

**Dźivo Droma**, released by Electronic Music Club as **EMC03**, is roughly two and a half hours of music assembled through years of sessions with Roma young people in Rotherham, England. The four long recordings splice local Roma musical traditions into makina, breakneck singeli-adjacent rhythms, rapping, and gloriously unruly electronics.

Rian Treanor is involved, as are collaborators including Lord Spikeheart, Roc of EVOL, Modern Institute, Petronn Sphene, and Porter Brook. But the interesting thing is that this isn't a producer's album with children supplying colorful samples. The club says hundreds of young people have participated in twice-weekly recording sessions, with performances at community events and festivals.

**All profits from the mixtape are earmarked for setting up a music studio at the youth club**, intended for the kids to use independently. That's a record serving as a piece of future infrastructure.

Noel Gardner's October 20 *Quietus* column calls attention to the actual sonic chaos rather than treating the participants as an uplifting footnote. The release itself is the proof of work: four sprawling sessions that sound like a scene inventing its own rules while the tape runs.

The distinction matters. Teaching somebody to make a track is useful. Helping them own the place where the next hundred tracks can be made is a different kind of accomplishment.

Sources: [Electronic Music Club's project notes](https://electronic-music.club/projects/record_label/), [the October 20 *Quietus* review](https://thequietus.com/quietus-reviews/new-weird-britain/new-weird-britain-in-review-for-october-by-noel-gardner/), and [the release with audio previews at Boomkat](https://boomkat.com/artists/relentless-youth-club/releases/cja2xymh/dzivo-droma).

## FOUND OBJECT · the cookbook with two front doors

Wellcome Collection holds a seventeenth-century English household manuscript, **MS.7577**, containing recipes for cakes, biscuits, macarons, medicinal waters, and historical remedies. Signatures in the volume include Ann de la Bere and Hester de la Bere; the catalog doesn't establish that they wrote every entry.

Its most useful oddity is architectural.

Part of the book was written in the normal direction. Another substantial section was **written upside down, starting from the back cover**. Between the two runs of recipes sit notes, signatures, and handwriting experiments.

A notebook wasn't necessarily a linear text awaiting a final chapter. It could be entered from whichever end was convenient, by different hands, for different purposes. The book has two beginnings and a messy interior where household knowledge accumulates.

The remedies are historical artifacts, not medical instructions. The interesting thing is how cooking, care, belief, measurement, and practical memory all occupied the same physical object.

**A notebook can be organized by how people use it rather than by a tidy taxonomy.** The object remembers its users' habits even when nobody wrote down the organizing principle.

Source: [Wellcome Collection, *Receipt Book, English, 17th century*, MS.7577](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/aztr3u4m).

## STUDIO FUEL · use an actual recipe as a scene clock

**Heart Through Stomach**, a solo journaling RPG by Gowanusaurus Rex, uses a real written recipe as the structure of a fictional evening. You imagine cooking for a guest. The recipe supplies the steps; a standard deck of cards supplies prompts; an optional block tower introduces physical tension.

The full version is $3, and a free printer-friendly version was made available in 2026.

The stealable idea is **borrow a process whose steps already make sense, then attach narrative uncertainty to them**.

Preparing ingredients is an opening scene. Heating the pan is commitment. Waiting is an opportunity for interruption. Serving is a confrontation with the person who has arrived. You don't have to manufacture pacing from scratch because the recipe has an order and a point of no return.

This is more interesting than rolling for random kitchen disasters. The meal can go perfectly while the relationship fails spectacularly.

Source: [*Heart Through Stomach* on itch.io](https://gowanusaurusrex.itch.io/heart-through-stomach).

## RABBIT HOLE · eight beats containing every possible three-beat phrase

Write this sequence in a circle:

`00010111`

Imagine **0** is a muted guitar hit and **1** is a sharp pick scrape. Play one symbol per beat and loop it.

Now examine every three-beat window, including the windows that wrap around the end:

`000 · 001 · 010 · 101 · 011 · 111 · 110 · 100`

That's **all eight possible three-symbol combinations**, each appearing exactly once.

This is a **de Bruijn sequence** of order three over two symbols. The general idea is a cyclic string that contains every possible fixed-length substring exactly once. It's established combinatorics, not a new result. For two symbols and windows of length three, eight positions are both necessary and sufficient.

The wonderfully counterintuitive part is that you don't need to list all eight triplets separately. They **overlap**. The end of one phrase is already the beginning of another.

Applications range from coding and experimental design to locating a position on a repeating strip. But it's also a tiny compositional machine: the loop exhausts every possible three-hit texture without ever sounding like a catalog.

Sources: [SageMath's de Bruijn sequence documentation](https://doc.sagemath.org/html/en/reference/combinat/sage/combinat/debruijn_sequence.html) and the [2026 De Bruijn Sequence and Universal Cycle Constructions project](https://debruijnsequence.org/db/home).

## CROSSWIRE · a dungeon written from both covers

Put the Wellcome notebook beside *Heart Through Stomach*.

Imagine a solo game played in a single physical notebook. Start from the front as a cook trying to feed a settlement. Start from the back, upside down, as a healer trying to understand an unexplained illness. Both characters use recipes as their scene structure, but they interpret the same ingredients differently.

Each session adds one entry from its respective end. The two narratives advance toward the center of the book.

**When they meet, the last remaining blank page must explain how the feast and the illness are connected.**

That's a campaign clock you can hold. It isn't a meter on a character sheet. It's the physical distance between two bodies of writing.

The historical notebook doesn't contain this game, of course. The collision simply suggests a mechanism worth stealing: **use the shape of a commonplace book to determine when the fiction must change**.

## 20 MINUTES · make the eight-beat impossible riff

Set a metronome to a comfortable pace, or just count evenly.

Assign two contrasting sounds: **0 = palm-muted string**, **1 = noisy scrape**. No guitar? Use a tabletop tap and a fingernail drag.

Play `00010111` as an eight-beat loop for one minute. Repeat it without changing the order. Record it if convenient.

Then write the eight overlapping three-beat patterns on a scrap of paper. Pick the one that feels most like a warning signal and circle it.

For the last five minutes, invent the reason an abandoned machine would play that exact three-beat warning, while the larger eight-beat loop continues forever.

One riff, one scrap, one unsettling explanation. Don't build a whole synthesizer monastery around it.

### TODAY'S RESIDUE

A mixtape can finance the room where its successors will be made. A household book can begin twice. A recipe can determine a story's pacing. An eight-beat loop can contain every possible three-beat phrase.

**Sometimes the structure isn't a container for the work. It's the most interesting thing the work does.**
