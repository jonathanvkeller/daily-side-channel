## FOUND OBJECT · A novel with another novel trapped inside

In 1966 Tom Phillips bought W. H. Mallock's obscure 1892 novel *A Human Document* for threepence and began transforming its pages through painting, collage and selective preservation of words. The resulting *A Humument* occupied him for decades. Even after the supposedly final 2016 edition, he returned to it, completing another 78 pages before his death in 2022.

The remarkable constraint is that the source page determines which words are available. It is not merely a canvas; it is a vocabulary that cannot be freely expanded. [Explore Phillips's project](https://www.tomphillips.co.uk/works/humument).

## STUDIO FUEL · Treat a recording as a page

Record thirty seconds of guitar, including the incidental noises. Duplicate the recording. In GarageBand, split and delete regions from the duplicate, leaving only five fragments in their original positions. Do not move them or add notes. Then make a second version using five entirely different fragments. The source recording becomes a fixed vocabulary, and editing becomes composition rather than cleanup.

## RABBIT HOLE · One cut, almost anything

The fold-and-cut theorem says that any finite planar pattern of straight-line cuts can be produced by folding an ideal sheet of paper flat and making one complete straight cut. Even disconnected polygons and polygons with holes are possible. The result was proved by Erik Demaine, Martin Demaine and Anna Lubiw around the turn of the millennium.

The intuition: folds reflect different edges onto a common cutting line. The difficult part is arranging all those reflections consistently. The theorem does not promise that the folds will be easy to execute with real cardstock.

[Explore the theorem and printable examples](https://erikdemaine.org/foldcut/).

## CROSSWIRE · A dungeon made by editing

Combine Phillips's fixed vocabulary with a solo dungeon crawl. Begin with a page of an old public-domain book. Divide it into six zones. Each room can be described only with words already present in its zone. Circle three words when you enter; use one as a feature, one as a danger and one as a possible resource. Leave unused words available for return visits. The page is both map and oracle. Unlike a random table, it has a finite local vocabulary that you gradually exhaust.

## 20 MINUTES · One page, two incompatible worlds

- 5 minutes: print a page from an old public-domain novel.
- 5 minutes: circle a sequence of words that suggests a mundane scene.
- 5 minutes: use a second copy to discover a disturbing scene without adding words or changing their order.
- 5 minutes: connect the surviving words with drawn lines and obscure the rest with black ink or hatching.

Keep both pages together. The same raw material has produced two different worlds.

## TODAY'S RESIDUE

A constraint need not make the result simple. Sometimes it merely relocates the complexity: from writing to selecting, from cutting to folding, from composing to editing.
