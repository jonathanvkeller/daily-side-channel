# Daily Side-Channel
**November 20, 2026 · No. 052**

*The Machine That Plays the Wrong Map*

A Costa Rican death-metal scene builds international connections out of borrowed rooms and overlapping bands. A mechanical violin plays without a violinist. And a solo cartographer discovers that the map is more interesting when it records the price of crossing.

Today's question: **what if a route is also a score?**

## THE SIGNAL · San José's death-metal network has escaped its borders

Brad Sanders's November 19 [Bandcamp Daily scene report](https://daily.bandcamp.com/scene-report/costa-rica-death-metal-scene-report) is worth reading for the infrastructure as much as the music.

San José's death-metal bands have increasingly reached international labels including Me Saco Un Ojo, Transcending Obscurity, and Nuclear Winter. But guitarist Justin Sánchez describes a scene still dependent on rehearsal spaces and home studios, with musicians circulating through several bands. This isn't a sudden invention of Costa Rican metal: the article traces predecessors back through Pseudostratiffied Epithelium, Corpse Garden, and others.

Two listening entrances: **Astriferous**, whose *Atavistic Unraveling* pushes old-school death metal toward angular dissonance, and **Perishing**, whose *Malicious Acropolis Unveiled* favors cavernous death/doom and negative space.

The useful observation: a small scene can export several distinct sounds because the people and resources overlap, not because everyone agrees on one style.

## FOUND OBJECT · a violin that plays itself

The early-twentieth-century **Mills Violano-Virtuoso** combines a self-playing violin and a piano in a cabinet. A perforated paper roll directs electromagnetic fingering mechanisms and motorized rollers that bow the violin strings. There are even provisions for staccato and vibrato.

This is not a recording pretending to be a performance. It is an actual string instrument being physically operated by a machine. Many examples were installed in restaurants, confectionery shops, and similar public spaces.

The wonderfully perverse detail: the paper roll encodes *gestures*, not just notes. The machine must know when to press, bow, lift, and shake.

[Examine the instrument and its rolls at Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/objects-of-intrigue-violano-virtuoso).

## STUDIO FUEL · write instructions for the gesture, not the pitch

Steal the Violano's distinction between sound and the action producing sound.

On one guitar string, write a four-symbol score: **X** = dead-string scrape, **O** = open-string ring, **/** = slide, **.** = abrupt mute. Write sixteen symbols before playing any of them.

Play the sequence three times, changing only duration and force. Don't choose notes. Let the physical gestures generate the material.

Then try playing the exact same score on a tabletop with your hands. If it still has an identity, you've composed a behavior rather than a riff.

## RABBIT HOLE · the map is the souvenir

In [*TRAILMARK* by Lilith](https://lilithsrpgs.itch.io/trailmark), a solo cartographer has twelve expeditions to survey the Unnamed Reach. Each sitting adds one hex to a hand-drawn map, with terrain, discoveries, hazards, rumors, and a cost for crossing. The designer describes the completed sheet as both the game and its keepsake.

The interesting design choice is not hexes. It's **mapping as an accumulating physical consequence**. Each new piece of geography is attached to an expedition that had a cost. The map isn't handed to you as objective truth; it grows through the act of trying to know a place.

The game's page lists a 22-page ruleset and four d66 oracle tables. This is a creator-described premise, not a claim based on a completed playthrough.

## CROSSWIRE · make a playable map-roll

Combine *TRAILMARK* with the Violano-Virtuoso.

Imagine a long strip of paper containing twelve connected locations. Under each location, punch or draw marks representing the action needed to cross it: scrape, tap, slide, silence. Feed the strip through a performance, one location per measure.

The **geography becomes a musical score**, but not by assigning a pretty melody to a landscape. It records the *friction* of traveling through it.

A collapsed bridge is a sudden mute. Loose scree is repeated scraping. A toll gate is a sharp repeated knock. When the expedition returns along the same route, play the strip backward.

You get two artifacts: a map of where you went and a noisy record of what it took to get there. The same idea could work as a zine with a perforated fold-out score.

## 20 MINUTES · score a terrible commute

Take one strip of paper and divide it into six segments.

1. **Five minutes:** invent six obstacles along a fictional route, without naming any settlements.
2. **Five minutes:** assign each obstacle a physical gesture: tap, scrape, ring, slide, knock, or silence.
3. **Five minutes:** perform the six gestures using one object or one guitar string. Repeat in reverse order.
4. **Five minutes:** write two sentences about the traveler. What changed on the outward journey, and why does the return sound different?

Stop there. One route, six gestures, and a physical score. No campaign encyclopedia required.

A small metal scene operates as a network of shared people and rooms. A mechanical violin converts holes in paper into physical gestures. A cartographer's sheet remembers the cost of crossing.
