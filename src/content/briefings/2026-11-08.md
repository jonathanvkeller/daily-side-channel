# DAILY SIDE-CHANNEL
*November 8, 2026 · No. 040*

## The Stone Has a Setlist

A record called *Theoretical Hard Core* runs philosophical concepts through breakcore. A Victorian musician assembles an actual rock band from stone. And a solo RPG makes the technology you need to escape the very thing hunting you.

**Today's question: what if the material you choose determines the trouble you get?**

## THE SIGNAL · no major signal today

**No major signal today.** Joe Muggs's November 6 [Bandcamp Daily electronic-music roundup](https://daily.bandcamp.com/best-electronic/the-best-electronic-music-on-bandcamp-october-2026) contains an outsider record that's more interesting than any headline I could force.

### Listening room · Hane, *Theoretical Hard Core*

Lancaster producer Hane has made a record that puts breakcore, rave and hi-NRG alongside titles such as “Phenomenal Consciousness,” “Inconsistent Triad,” and “The World as Will and Representation.” The sleeve features philosopher of mathematics Imre Lakatos. Joe Muggs describes it as gloriously untidy outsider art, full of philosophical references but requiring no prior seminar attendance.

The point isn't that rave suddenly needs a reading list. It's that philosophical language can be used as a compositional frame without turning the music into a lecture. Start with “Free Jazzstep,” then “The World as Will and Representation.” Treat the titles as provocations, not explanations.

[Find Hane in Bandcamp Daily's November 6 roundup](https://daily.bandcamp.com/best-electronic/the-best-electronic-music-on-bandcamp-october-2026).

## FOUND OBJECT · the original rock band was literally rock

In the nineteenth century, Keswick stonemason Joseph Richardson constructed a **rock harmonicon** from pieces of stone quarried from Skiddaw in England's Lake District. Its tuned stone bars were struck like a xylophone. Richardson's family performed with it under the magnificently direct name **Original Monstre Rock Band**.

An 1846 advertisement proudly promised “SOLID ROCK.” It meant it.

The interesting object isn't just a lithophone. It's a stonemason's intimate knowledge of a material being redirected into a musical instrument. A stone ordinarily valued for its durability and mass becomes a precisely tuned, resonating component.

[Read Betsy Golden Kellem's illustrated history at Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/original-rock-band-lithophone) (May 8, 2018).

## STUDIO FUEL · make the escape route attract the predator

[SWARM by Ryan LaLiberty](https://ryanlaliberty.itch.io/swarm) is a 21-page solo RPG combining a hexcrawl and a delve. You're a colonist trying to reach a rescue ship on planet Ramses. The Swarm consumes and corrupts flesh and circuitry, and is particularly attracted to machines.

That's a useful design inversion: **your best means of survival is also a beacon for the threat**.

A vehicle gets you across dangerous terrain but attracts attention. A scanner reveals the safe path but risks corruption. A powered door provides shelter and announces your location. The system doesn't need a second, unrelated complication to generate tension; the same tool produces both advantage and exposure.

Borrow that for a different game: every resource should have one benefit and one cost that grows out of the same physical property. Don't make the cost an arbitrary penalty. Make it the reason the resource works.

## RABBIT HOLE · stone is not acoustically dead

A lithophone is an *idiophone*: its own material vibrates to produce sound, unlike a guitar string or a drum membrane. The dimensions, stiffness, density, support points, and striking position all affect what you hear.

That's why the rock harmonicon isn't merely someone knocking stones together. It requires selection, shaping and tuning. The material's stubborn physical behavior becomes the instrument's pitch architecture.

The deeper rabbit hole is that **a material can be both a constraint and a score**. A designer can impose a pattern on stone, but stone's resonances decide which patterns can sing.

The [Original Monstre Rock Band history](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/original-rock-band-lithophone) is a good doorway into lithophones and other instruments that make their own bodies vibrate.

## CROSSWIRE · the instrument is the hazard

Put Richardson's rock harmonicon beside *SWARM*.

Imagine a settlement where the only reliable long-distance warning system is a set of tuned stone towers. Strike the correct sequence and a distant village can hear an approaching disaster.

Unfortunately, the thing approaching can also hear the towers.

The villagers don't face a simple choice between sounding the alarm and staying silent. They can change the rhythm, sacrifice one tower, or deliberately send a false pattern. Every message is both information and exposure.

A compact solo-RPG rule follows: **every successful signal advances the rescue clock and the danger clock by different amounts**. The player chooses what to communicate and accepts the cost of being understood.

The collision is useful because the danger doesn't arrive from a random encounter table. It comes directly from the communication system.

## 20 MINUTES · build a dangerous instrument

Take paper and a pencil. No need to purchase or strike actual stones.

1. **Five minutes:** draw an instrument with four parts made from one unlikely material: stone, glass, bone, or ice. Give each part a distinct sound or signal.
2. **Five minutes:** decide what the instrument communicates and who needs to hear it.
3. **Five minutes:** invent a danger that detects the same signal. Write one rule describing how louder or more complex messages increase the risk.
4. **Five minutes:** play one decision. Someone must send a warning but cannot safely send the entire message. What gets left out, and who pays for the omission?

Stop there. One instrument, one audience, one predator, one difficult choice.
