# DAILY SIDE-CHANNEL
November 7, 2026 · No. 039

*The Footnotes Have Teeth*

A punk record with citations in its track titles. A one-page game where you're the cursed weapon, not its owner. A nineteenth-century animation machine that makes a dozen drawings into an apparently living loop.

Today's question: **what if the thing that usually supports the story takes control of it?**

## THE SIGNAL · no major signal today

**No major signal today.** But yesterday's Bandcamp Daily album-of-the-day pick deserves attention for the way it combines music, research, and confrontation.

## LISTENING ROOM · the hardcore record is also a bibliography

**PISS — *you made me / skin on linen*** (Bandcamp Daily, November 6, 2026)

Vancouver hardcore quartet PISS make abrasive, compressed music with found speech and spoken-word elements. Katie Kurtz's [November 6 review](https://daily.bandcamp.com/album-of-the-day/piss-you-made-me-skin-on-linen-review) describes fifteen tracks that quote feminist writing and research rather than simply gesture at an intellectual influence. One track title itself is a bibliographic reference to an article by Rote Zora in a 1984 issue of *Emma*.

The record's subject matter includes sexual abuse and accountability, so this is not casual background listening. The compelling formal decision is that **the references are part of the music's architecture**, not just an explanatory paragraph in the liner notes.

An album can demand that you follow an argument outside the record. The citation becomes another instrument: a way of refusing to let the listener treat an accusation as merely an aesthetic effect.

[Read and listen through Bandcamp Daily](https://daily.bandcamp.com/album-of-the-day/piss-you-made-me-skin-on-linen-review).

## FOUND OBJECT · the weapon is trying to play you

**[You are now a cursed weapon!](https://doublemonk.itch.io/you-are-now-a-curses-weapon-a-solo-journaling-rpg)** by Pyry Qvick is a name-your-price, one-page solo journaling RPG made for the 2025 One-Page RPG Jam.

You aren't the adventurer carrying the sinister sword. You're the sword. Your goal is to break free by persuading or possessing your owner. Random tables shape the situation, and journal entries can be written in the hand of the owner or a witness.

The most useful reversal isn't the novelty of being an object. **Your protagonist has influence but lacks ordinary agency.** You cannot simply walk into the next room. You need another person to act, and their interests may not match yours.

That's an excellent engine for solo play: the character's power is inseparable from a relationship they cannot fully control.

## STUDIO FUEL · put the source inside the work

Take the PISS record's bibliographic approach without imitating its sound or subject.

Write a fictional document with a citation that is more interesting than the claim it supports. A village warning might cite a weather log; a spaceship safety manual might cite a court transcript; a recipe might cite an obituary.

Then write only the cited fragment.

The citation should **complicate** the original text, not confirm it. This turns research apparatus into an engine for narrative uncertainty.

For a solo RPG, you can use the same move when an oracle gives a result: instead of adding another fact to the world, add a source that disagrees with the last fact. Keep the discrepancy playable rather than immediately resolving it.

## RABBIT HOLE · a circle of drawings becomes a moving creature

The [phenakistoscope](https://publicdomainreview.org/collection/phenakistoscopes-1833/) was independently developed in 1832 by Joseph Plateau and Simon von Stampfer. Early commercial discs appeared in 1833.

The device put sequential drawings around a spinning disc. Looking through slots at the reflection in a mirror made the figures appear to move. A few stationary pictures became a repeating performance.

What matters isn't simply that it predates cinema. Its circular format makes **the ending feed straight back into the beginning**. A bow, leap, or transformation can continue forever without a conventional first or last frame.

The Public Domain Review's collection of early discs is a cabinet of beautifully strange loops. Look at how much personality the artists coaxed from tiny changes in posture.

The useful design question: what action becomes unsettling, funny, or meaningful when it cannot finish?

## CROSSWIRE · the sword has a bibliography

Combine the cursed-weapon game with the album's citations.

Imagine a sword that can only influence its wielder by recalling previous owners. Each command comes with a reference: a battle report, a confession, a merchant's receipt, a contradictory witness.

The sword cannot lie about what its sources say. But it can select which source to present.

The player is simultaneously the weapon and the editor of its history.

Now the mechanics are about **curation as manipulation**. The weapon's power isn't that it speaks with authority. It's that it decides which evidence the wielder gets to hear.

Borrow the phenakistoscope's loop: each new owner eventually reenacts one gesture from the previous owner, but changes one detail. A repeating curse becomes a historical pattern the player might finally break.

## 20 MINUTES · the unreliable footnote

1. **Five minutes:** invent an object that wants something from its owner. Give it one physical limitation.
2. **Five minutes:** write a three-sentence warning label for the object, including a footnote to an imaginary document.
3. **Five minutes:** write the footnoted document's surviving four lines. Make them contradict the warning in one specific way.
4. **Five minutes:** write a scene in which the owner notices the contradiction and must decide whether to obey the object anyway.

Stop before explaining which document is true. The disagreement is the playable material.

### TODAY'S RESIDUE

A punk record makes its reading list audible. A cursed weapon needs somebody else's hands. A spinning disc makes an ending become a beginning.

**The supporting material doesn't have to stay subordinate. Sometimes the footnote, the prop, or the repetition is the thing running the entire show.**
