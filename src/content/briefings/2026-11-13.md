# Daily Side-Channel · No. 045
*Friday, November 13, 2026*

**The Cube That Ate Its Larger Brother**

A Spanish underground compilation refuses to fit the usual post-punk family tree. A Latvian ensemble builds instruments from amplified junk. And mathematics proves that a cube can pass through a smaller cube, provided you stop treating the faces as sacred.

Today's question: **what changes when the shape of the opening matters more than the size of the thing?**

## THE SIGNAL · a recovered underground, not a new trend

No major signal today. The worthwhile recent release is *La Ola Nocturna: Experimental Songs and Deviant Pop from the Spanish '80s*, highlighted by Bandcamp Daily on November 12, 2026.

Curated by Löic Diaz Ronda and released by Les Disques Bongo Joe, the compilation gathers approximately 75 minutes of Spanish underground music, ranging from darkwave and oddball guitar pop to musique concrète and stubbornly minimalist no wave. Rather than treating an entire country's scene as a footnote to British or American post-punk, it lets the stylistic collisions speak.

Start with Mataparda's **“La llegada del cometa”**: deadpan voice, primitive guitar, hand percussion. Then try &Nil's **“Tilly”**, which moves toward drones and concrete sound. The productive surprise is how little these pieces need to share beyond their refusal to behave.

[Read Jim Allen's November 12 review and listen](https://daily.bandcamp.com/album-of-the-day/various-artists-la-ola-nocturna-experimental-songs-and-deviant-pop-from-the-spanish-80s-review).

## FOUND OBJECT · the zgamonium

An October 28 Bandcamp guide to the long-running experimental label ReR Megacorp includes **ZGA**, a group formed in Riga in 1984, later associated with Saint Petersburg. Their homemade **zgamoniums** use contact microphones to amplify vibrations from ordinary objects. On the 1996 album discussed in the guide, the result is clattering industrial-jazz percussion that feels both constructed and accidentally discovered.

A contact mic doesn't need the object to project sound through air the way an acoustic guitar does. It hears vibrations traveling through the object itself. The table, spring, can, or scrap of metal stops being a prop and becomes the sound source.

It's worth following the label guide beyond ZGA: ReR is an entire branching archive of music that ignores the polite boundary between rock, free improvisation, tape collage, and noise.

[Explore ReR Megacorp and ZGA in Bandcamp Daily's guide](https://daily.bandcamp.com/label-profile/rer-megacorp-label-profile).

## STUDIO FUEL · design an instrument with a veto

Borrow the zgamonium's spirit without building or buying anything. Choose one object and invent three ways to excite it: tap, scrape, and shake. For each action, assign one forbidden technique. If you tap, you cannot use rhythm. If you scrape, you cannot sustain. If you shake, you cannot repeat a gesture.

The trick is not to maximize sounds. It's to make the object's limitations compose for you. On guitar, translate the exercise to a single string: one gesture for attack, one for friction, one for decay. Don't add chords until the three gestures have formed an argument.

## RABBIT HOLE · a larger cube through a smaller cube

**Prince Rupert's cube** is a real, established result in recreational geometry: a cube can have a square tunnel cut through it wide enough to admit another cube whose side is about **1.06066 times larger**. The tunnel must be angled through the original cube; it isn't simply a hole punched squarely through one face.

The point is not that volume has become negotiable. Removing material creates a passage, and the geometry of a three-dimensional object permits an opening larger than a casual face-on view suggests.

This is the kind of puzzle that rewards a sketch, a paper model, or a deliberate attempt to disprove it before reading the construction.

[Prince Rupert's cube at Wolfram MathWorld](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/PrinceRupertsCube.html).

## CROSSWIRE · the dungeon has a resonant aperture

Put Prince Rupert's cube beside ZGA's amplified objects. Imagine a sealed underground settlement where doors are not sized for the objects passing through them. They are tuned to **orientations and resonances**.

A cargo cube too large for the visible doorway fits if rotated correctly. But the rotation makes a metal frame sing, alerting everything listening through the rock. Every successful passage produces an audible signature.

For a solo RPG, replace a generic lock-picking roll with two questions: *What orientation makes the passage possible?* and *What hears the resulting vibration?* The geometry supplies the puzzle; the sound supplies the consequence.

## 20 MINUTES · build an impossible customs gate

1. **Five minutes:** Draw a cube-shaped cargo container and a smaller-looking doorway. Invent a strange orientation that might allow passage.
2. **Five minutes:** Give the doorway three materials. Decide what each sounds like when touched or struck.
3. **Five minutes:** Invent an official whose job is to identify contraband by the sounds objects make while passing through.
4. **Five minutes:** Write a short scene in which a traveler gets an oversized object through the gate, only to discover the sound has identified something they wanted hidden.

Stop before explaining the whole civilization. You have a physical puzzle, a surveillance method, and one consequential mistake.
